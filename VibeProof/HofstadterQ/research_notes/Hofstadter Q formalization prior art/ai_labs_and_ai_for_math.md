## 1. Bottom line: has any frontier lab, AI-for-math company or AI-assisted effort worked on "is Q defined for all n?"

### Takeaway
No frontier lab or AI-for-math company has published, announced or claimed any proof attempt, partial result or targeted study of Hofstadter's Q-sequence or Erdős #422. Every documented AI activity on #422 itself is small and indirect:
- **LLM audits (Sept 2026).** Epoch AI's Tom Adamczewski ran LLM audits of the Lean *statement* in google-deepmind/formal-conjectures. They proved small lemmas in Lean and showed that one variant is secretly equivalent to the open totality question.
- **One rejected AI "solve" PR** (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Mar 2026).
- **Two third-party LLM sweeps** (GPT-5.2 Pro / Deep Research, Jan 2026; "agentic-erdos", Mar 2026). They produced only a literature summary and computations.
- **An inferred, unreported inclusion** of the then-opaque #422 statement in DeepMind's formal proof-search run over the 353 formal-conjectures Erdős statements as of early February 2026. It was not solved.

AI-assisted work on *relatives* is more substantial, but all of it is human-led:
- Two independent 2026 proofs that the parity-perturbed Q is well-defined (Mantovanelli; Cloitre), both disclosing ChatGPT use.
- Erdős #424, Hofstadter's ab−1 problem, solved with GPT-5.6 Pro and formalized with Codex.
- A Claude-assisted Lean formalization of the 2026 human results on Erdős #423.

### Inferences

## 2. Google DeepMind: formal-conjectures `ErdosProblems/422.lean` history, issues and PRs, benchmarks; AlphaProof / AlphaEvolve / Gemini Deep Think

### Takeaway
422.lean was added by community contributor Anirudh Rao (PR #692: opened 2025-09-28, merged 2025-11-11). From then until 2026-09-12 it defined Q as an opaque `partial def`, with no equations in Lean's logic. Salvatore Mercuri (a Formal Conjectures co-author) replaced it with the `IsHofstadterQ` predicate in PR #5791 (merged 2026-09-12), prompted by an AI-generated issue. No status has ever changed (still "research open"). The only "solve" PR was an AI-generated loophole attempt, closed the same day.

Since the fix, an open AI-audit issue (#6544) shows that `erdos_422.variants.eventually_const` is *logically equivalent* to the negation of totality. So the repo unintentionally *does* contain the well-definedness question, but disguised.

#422 is not in DeepMind's frozen FC100 benchmark subsets, and no DeepMind paper mentions it. It was very likely among the 353 statements (repo state as of early Feb 2026) in DeepMind's formal proof-search run, in its broken opaque form.

### Inferences
- **Correction to the session notes.** The current `IsHofstadterQ` form, its uniqueness docstring and the f3/f4 tests are only four weeks old (2026-09-12). From 2025-11-11 to 2026-09-12 the four statements referred to an opaque function. That includes the period of DeepMind's Feb 2026 run.
- **Extension of the session's "missing well-definedness statement" gap.** Under #6544's (Lean-checked) argument, `erdos_422.variants.eventually_const` currently reads `answer(sorry) ↔ ¬∃ f, IsHofstadterQ f`. So the open totality question *is* in the repo, but by accident and under a misleading docstring. If the audit's proposed fix is adopted (`answer(False) ↔ ∃ f, IsHofstadterQ f ∧ EventuallyConst f atTop`, recategorized as textbook), the repo would again have no totality statement. The Opus audit also observed that if Q dies, `erdos_422` and `surjective` both hold vacuously and "give two incompatible answers".
- **DeepMind's role is infrastructural only.** DeepMind maintainers reviewed and merged community and AI-audit-driven changes. I found no DeepMind researcher proof attempt, status change, blog or paper on #422 or Q. It is very likely, but not documented, that #422's opaque statements were among the 353 Feb 2026 targets. No agent could have proved anything meaningful about them, and no result was reported.

## 6. Mantovanelli (arXiv 2603.29622) and the 2026 perturbed-Q literature: AI assistance and formalization

## 9. Stephen Wolfram's "Nestedly Recursive Functions" (September 2024) and follow-ups

### Takeaway
The essay (27 Sep 2024) is a ruliological exploration, not a proof effort. It mostly uses the convention f[n]=1 for n≤0, which makes totality trivial. It surveys the P, T and S families and discusses totality, termination and primitive recursiveness only qualitatively. It contains no automated or formal proofs and no AI or LLM use.

The Wolfram Institute republished it under "Output". I found no Institute follow-up (2024–2026) on nested recursion, totality or Hofstadter Q, apart from a secondhand 2024 Summer Research project title.

### Cited Findings
These come from the primary page, read through a WebFetch summary; the final ~15% (Thanks and Bibliography) was not read. — [Nestedly Recursive Functions](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/09/nestedly-recursive-functions/)
- [P] Dated September 27, 2024.
- [P] The "standard" initial condition is f[n] = 1 for n ≤ 0, though early examples keep f[1] = f[2] = 1. The essay notes that some choices lead to "f[n] = f[n]" situations and could be "reasons that f[n] might not be a 'total function', defined for all n".
- [P] Families: P (f[n] := a + f[n – b f[n – c]]), T (f[n] := a f[n – b f[n – c]]) and S (f[n] := n – f[f[n – a] – b]). It recalls a 1993 enumeration checked "out to 10 million terms".
- [P] It notes that the literature is "mostly concentrated very specifically around 'Conway's Challenge Sequence', Hofstadter's Q function, or very similar 'meta Fibonacci' generalizations".
- [P] No mention of FindEquationalProof, automated theorem proving, formal verification or LLMs. "AI" appears only historically (McCarthy). The open questions it raises include computational universality, emulation via initial conditions, consistency of definitions, and primitive-recursive answerability.
- [P] The Wolfram Institute "Recursion" tag page lists only this essay (2024-09-27) and "Expression Evaluation and Fundamental Physics" (2023-09-09). — [wolframinstitute.org/output/tag/Recursion](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/tag/Recursion); [Institute copy](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/nestedly-recursive-functions)
- [S] A Wolfram Summer Research Institute alumni page lists a 2024 project, "Exploring Call Graphs of Nestedly Recursive Functions" (Thomas Adler). This is from a search snippet only. — [alumni page](https://education.wolfram.com/summer-research-institute/alumni/2024/thomas-adler/)
- [P] Hacker News submissions of the essay: 2024-09-27 (id 41674295), 2024-09-28 (41677740) and 2024-10-01 (41707144), found via the HN Algolia API. — [HN 41674295](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41674295)
