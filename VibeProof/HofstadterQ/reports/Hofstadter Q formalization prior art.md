# Hofstadter's Q eludes every prover and lab

No one has proved that Hofstadter's Q-sequence is defined for all n, and no frontier lab, AI-for-math company, formal-methods group or mathematician has published a credible plan to. Forty-nine years on, the only published theorem about Q's behavior is Golomb's conditional result that a convergent Q(n)/n must tend to 1/2. The 3·10¹⁰-term computational record (Carr, 2023) has no published method, and Wolfram's 2024 survey of nested recursion sidesteps the question by setting f(n) = 1 for n ≤ 0. AI work on Erdős #422 has touched only its Lean *statement*. Epoch AI's LLM audits found that Google DeepMind's formal-conjectures file had defined Q as an opaque `partial def` for ten months (fixed 12 September 2026), and they machine-checked a few structural lemmas. DeepMind's own proof-search campaigns attempted #422 only in that unusable form. No proof assistant states Q's totality directly; it survives in formal-conjectures only by accident, inside a variant equivalent to "Q dies". Nor does any proof assistant hold a totality proof for any relative where totality takes real work. The year's real news is a relative, the parity-perturbed Q̃(n) = Q̃(n−Q̃(n−1)) + Q̃(n−Q̃(n−2)) + (−1)ⁿ. It gained two independent, ChatGPT-assisted, unrefereed totality proofs, neither machine-checked, and both work because the perturbation makes the sequence secretly slow, a structure Q lacks. A Lean 4 + Mathlib project here adds the most in three ways. It can make Q's totality a canonical statement in Hofstadter's own convention and upstream it. It can formalize the few theorems truly about the Q-recurrence, namely Golomb's conditional limit and Fox's death theorem for starts 1..N. And it can deliver the first machine-checked totality proof for a Q-type recurrence whose totality was genuinely open: the V-sequence as a warm-up, then Q̃ via Cloitre's proof.

## History and context: Wolfram's 2024 essay pads away Hofstadter's question

The Q-sequence entered mathematics through letters and problem books rather than papers. Hofstadter described it to Neil Sloane in 1977 ([Hofstadter–Sloane correspondence](https://oeis.org/A006336/a006336.pdf)) and printed it in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* ([OEIS A005185](https://oeis.org/A005185)). Erdős and Graham's 1980 problem book turned it into what erdosproblems.com lists as #422, "Asked by Hofstadter", with the question "Does f(n) miss infinitely many integers? What is its behaviour?" ([Erdős #422](https://www.erdosproblems.com/422)). Guy's *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory* lists it as E31 ([MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/HofstadtersQ-Sequence.html)). Experimental work came next: Golomb's "Discrete chaos" manuscript from around 1990, preserved as a scan on OEIS; Pinn's statistical studies from 1998; and a page in Wolfram's *A New Kind of Science* (2002, p. 129) ([OEIS A005185](https://oeis.org/A005185); [Pinn 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/chao-dyn/9803012)).

Stephen Wolfram's essay "Nestedly Recursive Functions" (27 September 2024) is the most recent long-form survey of this territory, and the natural background for this project. The Wolfram Institute republished it ([Wolfram 2024](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/09/nestedly-recursive-functions/); [Institute copy](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/nestedly-recursive-functions)). It retells the history from Hofstadter's 1977 letter, which already singled Q out as the wild member of its family, through Golomb's unfinished paper. It notes that the literature is "mostly concentrated very specifically around 'Conway's Challenge Sequence', Hofstadter's Q function, or very similar 'meta Fibonacci' generalizations". Its own contribution is "ruliology", Wolfram's term for systematically exploring what simple rules do. It surveys whole families of nested recurrences, which it labels P, T and S: f[n] := a + f[n − b f[n − c]], f[n] := a f[n − b f[n − c]], and f[n] := n − f[f[n − a] − b]. It also recalls a 1993 enumeration checked "out to 10 million terms". Wolfram argues that such functions show so much computational irreducibility that traditional mathematics can say little about them, so studying them means systematic computational exploration. He reflects that in 1981 he could have recognized in Q the phenomenon he later made famous with rule 30.

For a formalization project, the essay matters most because of its convention for values at n ≤ 0. Its "standard" initial condition sets f[n] = 1 for n ≤ 0, although early examples keep Hofstadter's f[1] = f[2] = 1 ([Wolfram 2024](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/09/nestedly-recursive-functions/)). Under any positive padding every value is at least 1, so every lookback n − f(n−1) is strictly below n, and a one-line induction shows the recursion is total. The padded sequence agrees with Q on every prefix where Q is defined, but the totality question vanishes.

Three conventions circulate, and only one poses the open problem. Hofstadter's own leaves Q undefined at n ≤ 0, so the sequence dies when a lookback leaves [1, n−1]; Fox calls this "weak death" ([Fox 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01365)). Ruskey's Fibonacci solution and Fox's quasipolynomial families read zeros at nonpositive indices ([Fox 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06484)). Wolfram's essay reads ones. The essay acknowledges the issue in passing: some setups produce "f[n] = f[n]" situations that are possible "reasons that f[n] might not be a 'total function', defined for all n". It lists the consistency of such definitions among its open questions, alongside computational universality, emulating one recurrence by another through initial conditions, and whether questions about these functions have primitive recursive answers. It does not pursue Hofstadter's version.

The essay also marks where the Wolfram corpus stops. It contains no proofs, formal or automated, never mentions theorem provers, and uses "AI" only in a historical aside ([Wolfram 2024](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/09/nestedly-recursive-functions/)). The Institute's "Recursion" tag lists only this essay and a 2023 piece on expression evaluation ([Institute "Recursion" tag](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/tag/Recursion)). The only follow-up we found is a 2024 Summer Research project on call graphs of nestedly recursive functions, known only from a search snippet ([Summer Research alumni page](https://education.wolfram.com/summer-research-institute/alumni/2024/thomas-adler/)). The essay therefore supplies stakes, vocabulary and a reservoir of related recurrences. But any Lean statement built on it must replace its padding with Hofstadter's partial convention, or it will prove a triviality. The sections on formal artifacts and Lean targets below return to this point.

## After 49 years, Q's lone theorem is a conditional limit

Every serious source reports that the most basic question is still open. OEIS states that **"it is not even known if this sequence is defined for all positive n"** ([OEIS A005185](https://oeis.org/A005185)). Balamohan, Kuznetsov and Tanny wrote in 2007 that "virtually nothing has been proved about the enigmatic behavior of this sequence" ([BKT 2007](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Tanny/tanny3.html)). Nathan Fox's 2017 thesis calls the question of whether Q dies **"the primary and most classical open question about nested recurrences"** ([Fox thesis](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/Theses/NathanFoxThesis.pdf)). Both 2026 papers that revived the area repeat the verdict. Cloitre writes that "virtually nothing has been proved rigorously about Q itself" ([Cloitre v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06237v1)), and Pinn that "rigorous results exist only for the 'tame' or 'slow' sequences" ([Pinn 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17509v1)).

Very little is established. "Defined for all n" means every lookback index stays at least 1, and the literature agrees this comes down to one inequality. The sequence dies at index n exactly when Q(n−1) ≥ n ([Fox 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01365)), so **totality is equivalent to Q(n) ≤ n for all n**; the lower bound 1 ≤ Q(n) is automatic ([Deane–Gentile](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13854)). Beyond that, Fox's thesis states that "all that is known rigorously is that if lim Q(n)/n exists, it must equal one half", crediting Golomb's unpublished manuscript ([Fox thesis](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/Theses/NathanFoxThesis.pdf)).

Q is plainly not "slow" (a slow sequence's increments are all 0 or 1): Q(12) = 8 while Q(11) = 6 ([Fox 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08244)). Even its two-step differences wander, with Q(24) − Q(22) = 4 and Q(26) − Q(24) = −2 ([OEIS A005185](https://oeis.org/A005185)). So none of the slowness machinery that tames Q's relatives applies. Not even unboundedness has a published proof. The Erdős question about missed values rests on data alone. One forum computation to 1.1·10⁸ found that 7 never appears and that about 14% of values are skipped, which the poster calls "purely empirical" ([Erdős #422 forum](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/422)).

There are more heuristics than theorems. In 1999 Pinn grouped the terms into generations of 2ᵏ members whose parents lie one or two generations back, "Fibonacci type on a logarithmic scale". He found that the fluctuations around n/2 have an amplitude scaling like n^0.88 ([Pinn 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/chao-dyn/9803012)). Returning to the problem in 2026, he measured generation lengths scaling like (2 − η)ᵏ with η ≈ 0.006. The generation boundaries equal 3·2ᵏ⁻² exactly for 3 ≤ k ≤ 10 and then drift below ([Pinn 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17509v1)).

Around 1999 Hofstadter and Huber considered the recurrences Q(n − Q(n−r)) + Q(n − Q(n−s)) started from all ones. They conjectured that only (r,s) = (1,2), (1,4) and (2,4) live forever. The (2,4) case "appears to display even more inscrutably wild behavior" and is equally unproved ([BKT 2007](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Tanny/tanny3.pdf)).

Experts expect Q to survive. BKT judged it "highly unlikely that the sequence dies", and Fox wrote that "empirical evidence suggests that it lives forever". Yet **no one has proposed a credible proof strategy**. The closest is Cloitre's April 2026 conjecture that |Q(n) − Q̃(n)| = O(n/√log n), which "would imply Q(n)/n → 1/2 and the well-definedness of Q for all n". It rests only on numerics up to 2·10⁵ and is absent from his later abstracts ([Cloitre v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06237v1)).

Results on nearby recurrences show where the difficulty lies. Fox proved that the same recurrence started from 1, 2, …, N **dies for N = 8, 11, 12 and every N ≥ 14**, after exactly N + 28 terms when N ≥ 21; the proof computes 28 terms symbolically in N. The cases N ∈ {4, …, 7, 9, 10, 13} are still open after 3·10⁷ terms, and N = 2 and 3 reproduce Q itself ([Fox 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01365)). Golomb's start 3, 2, 1 gives a solution with period-3 structure, each residue class following a simple linear formula, and it provably lives ([OEIS A244477](https://oeis.org/A244477)). Ruskey's Fibonacci embedding and Fox's quasipolynomial solutions of every degree exist only under zero padding, one of the conventions that change the question (see the history section above) ([Fox 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06484)). Celaya and Ruskey built one nested recurrence for which it is undecidable whether a given initial condition yields a total sequence ([Celaya–Ruskey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0586)). That explains why each case needs its own argument, but it says nothing about whether Q in particular is total.

### The 3·10¹⁰ record is unverified and cannot decide the question

The record has stood since mid-2023. OEIS credits Roman Pearce with existence for n ≤ 10¹⁰ (August 2014) and M. Eric Carr with **n ≤ 3·10¹⁰ (July 2023)** ([OEIS A005185](https://oeis.org/A005185)). Carr's submission note says only "Computed the first thirty billion terms using result caching; please let me know if specific evidence is needed" ([OEIS history](https://oeis.org/history?seq=A005185)). Neither record publishes code, hardware, runtime or data, and no one has independently reproduced either. Every other computation is smaller: Hofstadter's privately reported "billions of terms" ([BKT 2007](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Tanny/tanny3.pdf)), the forum's 1.1·10⁸, and Agentic Erdős's 2·10⁶ ([Agentic Erdős](https://agentic-erdos.vercel.app/data/ep422.json)).

Computation checked inside a proof assistant lags far behind. This project's experiments reached about 10⁴ terms with kernel-only `decide +kernel` (53.7 s, 8.2 GB), and 10⁷ with `native_decide`, which trusts the compiler ([QKernelDecide.lean](../experiments/QKernelDecide.lean); [QNativeDecide.lean](../experiments/QNativeDecide.lean)). Epoch's audit reports an unpublished Lean check to 2·10⁵ ([Epoch audit record](https://github.com/tadamcz/fc-review-results/blob/main/data/aea251bb26/files/ErdosProblems/422.json)). Totality is a statement of the form "every n passes a finite check" (a Π⁰₁ statement), so no finite computation can prove it; computation can only refute it. Pushing the record further adds evidence, but never a proof.

## AI labs audited Q's Lean statement but never attacked Q

No frontier lab or AI-for-math company has published, announced or claimed a proof attempt, partial result or targeted study of Q or Erdős #422. That stands out against the volume of AI work on Erdős problems. Terence Tao's AI-contributions wiki logged 538 problem rows across GPT-5, Aristotle, Gemini, Claude, AlphaProof, AxiomProver, Seed Prover and others before it was frozen on 30 June 2026, and none of them is #422 ([Tao's AI wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems)). The #422 page still shows **zero proof claims** ([Erdős #422](https://www.erdosproblems.com/422)).

**Google DeepMind's role has been to host the problem, not to attack it.** A community contributor added #422 to DeepMind's formal-conjectures repository in November 2025, defining Q as a `partial def` ([PR #692](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/692)). In Lean a `partial def` becomes an opaque constant with no equations, so for ten months the four #422 statements referred to an unspecified function ([issue #5649](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/issues/5649)). During review, co-author Salvatore Mercuri considered the other reading of "termination", reaching an index where f(n) cannot be defined. He judged it "already captured in the definition", so no totality statement was added ([PR #692](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/692)).

DeepMind's formal proof-search agent later ran on "all Lean statements of Erdős problems available in the Formal Conjectures repository as of early February 2026 – 353 problems in total". That snapshot included the opaque #422 file. The run used about 27.5 TPU-hours per problem and solved nine, and #422 is never named ([arXiv 2605.22763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763)). A DeepMind repository of AlphaProof Nexus results lists `erdos_422`, `.variants.surjective` and `.variants.eventually_const` as attempted. It publishes only successful proofs and has no #422 file. Those attempts predate the September fix, so they faced the opaque constant, about which nothing nontrivial is provable ([AlphaProof Nexus attempted list](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results/blob/main/erdos_problems_attempted.txt)). The OEIS campaign in the same paper deliberately selected "not famous open problems" ([arXiv 2605.22763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763)). DeepMind's Aletheia and AlphaEvolve papers never mention Q ([arXiv 2601.22401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22401); [arXiv 2511.02864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02864)). #422 is also absent from the repository's frozen FC100 benchmark subsets ([Subsets](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/tree/main/FormalConjectures/Subsets)).

**Epoch AI did the only substantive AI work on #422, and it checked whether the Lean statement matches the problem.** Senior research engineer Tom Adamczewski ([profile](https://github.com/tadamcz)) ran four LLM audits of the #422 file between 7 and 23 September 2026, under the model labels GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5. They were part of a review of the whole repository that has so far produced 175 issues labelled `ai-audit` ([fc-review-results](https://github.com/tadamcz/fc-review-results); [ai-audit issues](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/issues?q=label%3Aai-audit)). The audit's report on the opaque definition prompted Mercuri's fix on **12 September 2026**, which replaced the `partial def` with the predicate `IsHofstadterQ` ([PR #5791](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/5791)).

A later Opus 5.5 run, costing $8.54 and 56.5 minutes, produced a sorry-free Lean proof that no solution is eventually constant: if f(n) = c for all large n, the recurrence forces c = 2c. It follows that the `eventually_const` variant is equivalent to "no solution exists". The resulting **issue #6544 remains open** ([Epoch audit record](https://github.com/tadamcz/fc-review-results/blob/main/data/aea251bb26/files/ErdosProblems/422.json); [issue #6544](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/issues/6544)). Earlier runs reported three more results: uniqueness of solutions, a guarded computation to 10⁴, and the bound f(n) ≤ n. That bound makes the `growth_rate` variant provable with the answer n ([audit run 782312817c](https://github.com/tadamcz/fc-review-results/blob/main/data/782312817c/files/ErdosProblems/422.json)), and a human contributor had separately flagged that variant as self-answering ([issue #4923](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/issues/4923)). Epoch's curated benchmarks exclude Q. The 68 problems of FrontierMath Erdős skip #422 ([arXiv 2609.25050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25050)), and FrontierMath: Open Problems contains no nested recurrence ([FM:OP](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems)).

Other labs appear only through third parties. OpenAI's October 2025 GPT-5 "Erdős problems" episode, which TechCrunch covered as an embarrassment over rediscovered literature, did not involve #422 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/19/openais-embarrassing-math/); [Tao's AI wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems)). None of OpenAI's Erdős-related papers mentions Q ([arXiv 2511.16072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16072); [arXiv 2603.29961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29961)). Neel Somani's independent GPT-Erdos sweep gave #422 to GPT-5.2 Pro and Deep Research. It got back a literature summary concluding "it is *not proved* that the sequence is well-defined for all n" ([GPT-Erdos #422](https://github.com/neelsomani/gpt-erdos/blob/main/data/solutions/422/candidate_solution.md)).

Anthropic's Claude appears in a March 2026 pull request "generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6 in OpenCode harness". It applied a "`research formally solved` loophole pattern" and was closed unmerged the same day ([PR #2917](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/2917)). A third-party set of submissions to Harmonic's Aristotle prover marks #422 "AVOID" ([submission file](https://github.com/kavanaghpatrick/aristotle-math-problems/blob/main/submissions/erdos/tier5/erdos_422_erdos_422.lean)). Meta's only contact with the family is a definition for Conway-like sequences in its atlas-lean library ([atlas-lean](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/blob/main/MathlibExt/NumberTheory/ConwayHofstadterLikeSequence.lean)). Searches restricted to the sites of Math Inc, Axiom, ByteDance Seed, DeepSeek, Mistral, xAI and Morph found nothing, consistent with the lab-by-lab account in Tao's wiki ([AI companies wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/How-have-AI-companies-been-invested-in-Erd%C5%91s-problems%3F)).

Agentic research trackers are only beginning to queue the problem. Agentic Erdős checked it to 2·10⁶ ([Agentic Erdős](https://agentic-erdos.vercel.app/data/ep422.json)). A Grand Challenge task packet created on 9 October 2026 assigns a 35-minute "source audit and bounded research design" ([MATH-PROGRAMME #1692](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1692)). Lean's public Zulip archive has no thread on Q, A005185 or #422 ([Zulip archive](https://github.com/leanprover-community/archive)).

AI has made a real difference only on Q's relatives, always with humans in charge. For Erdős #424, Hofstadter's ab − 1 set, Samuel Korsky proved positive lower density using GPT-5.6 Pro to search and check data, and Boris Alexeev formalized the proof in Lean with Codex ([arXiv 2608.07910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07910); [Erdos424.lean](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/main/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos424.lean)). Erdős #423, the Hofstadter consecutive-sum sequence, was settled by Q. Tang ([arXiv 2603.09939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09939)). It was then formalized with Claude's help in a development that first proves its defining predicate has exactly one solution, "so the statements are not vacuous" ([#423 Lean](https://github.com/baobingzhang/jsp-000348-erdos423-lean)). That is exactly the step #422 cannot take, because for Q, existence *is* the open problem.

Both 2026 proofs for the perturbed Q̃ disclose ChatGPT use. Mantovanelli's covers "the development and checking of proof ideas, and the drafting and refactoring of verification code" ([arXiv 2603.29622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29622); [arXiv 2604.06237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06237)). Campbell and Cloitre used Claude for their paper on meta-automatic sequences ([arXiv 2602.23395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23395)). Cloitre, Ma and Zhang built their September 2026 Lean 4 development of a nested recurrence with Claude, GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Astra ([arXiv 2609.33421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33421)).

## Every formal artifact stops at Q's definition

Mathlib contains nothing on Q or any meta-Fibonacci sequence. Its only Hofstadter content is the MIU system from *Gödel, Escher, Bach* ([Mathlib MIU](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Archive/MiuLanguage/Basic.lean)). The one formal encoding of Q's open problem is the formal-conjectures file ([422.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/422.lean)). Since September it defines `IsHofstadterQ (f : ℕ+ → ℕ+)` by f 1 = 1, f 2 = 1, and, for n > 2, the side conditions f(n−1) < n and f(n−2) < n together with the recurrence. It proves f 3 = 2 and f 4 = 3. It states four open questions of the form `answer(sorry) ↔ ∀ f, IsHofstadterQ f → …`, where `answer(sorry)` stands for the unknown answer. Its docstring asserts, without proof, that at most one function satisfies the predicate and that one exists iff Q is total ([PR #5791](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/5791)).

**No statement asserts `∃ f, IsHofstadterQ f`**, so every question holds vacuously if Q dies. The Opus audit notes that `erdos_422` and `surjective` would then "give two incompatible answers". Totality survives only inside `eventually_const`. The audit proposed adding `well_defined : answer(sorry) ↔ ∃ f, IsHofstadterQ f`, but that is unmerged. #6544's suggested restatement of `eventually_const` would remove even the accidental copy unless `well_defined` is adopted alongside it ([issue #6544](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/issues/6544)).

The deepest formal work on any Hofstadter recurrence is Pierre Letouzey's development in Rocq (formerly Coq), begun in 2015 and updated as recently as 9 October 2026. It covers G, H and the generalized F_k family through numeration systems, frequencies, discrepancy and pointwise order. A 2026 paper in the *Journal of Integer Sequences* comes with "a certified formalization of all these results" ([hofstadter_g](https://github.com/letouzey/hofstadter_g); [arXiv 2410.00529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00529)). Its techniques carry over to Q: a relation recording input–output pairs, a `Program Fixpoint` with subset types, fuel, and a fast table-based version proved equal to the specification. But its totality proof rests on the lemma `G_le : G n a -> a <= n`, "used for proving that G is a total function" ([FunG.v](https://github.com/letouzey/hofstadter_g/blob/main/FunG.v)). For Q, the matching bound Q(m) ≤ m *is* the open problem.

Every system hits the same wall. Why3 and Dafny prove that Hofstadter's Female/Male functions terminate by putting 1 ≤ f(n) ≤ n in the postcondition ([Why3 example](https://github.com/AdaCore/why3/blob/master/examples/mutual_recursion.mlw)). Isabelle's function package would express totality as a domain predicate, but its documentation illustrates nested recursion with McCarthy 91, never Q ([Isabelle Functions.thy](https://github.com/isabelle-prover/mirror-isabelle/blob/master/src/HOL/Examples/Functions.thy)). Code searches of Metamath, HOL4, HOL Light, ACL2, PVS, Mizar and Agda found nothing, though GitHub's patchy search index makes those negatives weak.

Termination benchmarks are the wrong home. The Termination Problem Database has no Hofstadter-style entry ([TPDB](https://github.com/TermCOMP/TPDB)), and the question is not really about termination. Under the essay's positive padding the recursion terminates by a one-line induction. Under Hofstadter's convention the question is a safety property: the recursion must never read an index ≤ 0. Under zero padding, a term can even be forced to read itself.

Lean's tooling makes clear what is hard. `partial_fixpoint`, shipped in Lean v4.17.0 in March 2025, accepts nested recursion inside `Option`, with `none` marking non-termination ([Lean reference](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Definitions/Recursive-Definitions/); [v4.17.0](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/releases/tag/v4.17.0)). Joachim Breitner's standard McCarthy 91 example shows the recipe: define the function in `Option`, prove `isSome` by a separate induction, then extract a total function. He explains that ordinary well-founded recursion is "doomed" for such functions "because we'd have to prove something about the function we are defining" ([Zulip archive](https://github.com/leanprover-community/archive/blob/master/zulip_json/270676-lean4/McCarthy.2091.20function.20using.20partial_fixpoint.json)).

Q is easier to *define* than McCarthy 91. Once both lookbacks are guarded to lie in [1, n−1], every call goes to a smaller index. So plain well-founded recursion into `Option` compiles without `partial_fixpoint`, and so does this project's padded definition with `termination_by n` ([QDefinition.lean](../experiments/QDefinition.lean)). That padding differs in kind from the essay's. It is an implementation device, sound as long as the theorem asserts that the padding is never read, not a convention that redefines the question. Either way, **the open problem is the `isSome` lemma**, not termination.

Two smaller findings matter for anyone building on these artifacts. First, AI benchmarks have embedded the open problem without flagging it. FVAPPS task 2638, carried into the vericoding benchmark, asks for a Lean function with Q(0) = 0 that satisfies positivity and the recurrence with truncated subtraction ([LF2638](https://github.com/Beneficial-AI-Foundation/vericoding-benchmark/blob/main/specs/LF2638_specs.lean); [FVAPPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05714)). A sorry-free solution would have to show that this zero-padded Q never returns 0. Q's totality implies that, and no known method proves it any more easily. FormalConjectures-Bench turned the opaque #422 statements into prove-or-refute tasks with no reference answer ([FC-Bench task](https://github.com/AllenGrahamHart/FormalConjectures-Bench/tree/main/tasks-v2/erdosproblems-422-eventually-const-refute)).

Second, the relatives that *have* been formalized are exactly those whose totality is trivial: Letouzey's G/H/F_k, Cloitre–Ma–Zhang's A076502, sequencelib's synthesized G and Female ([sequencelib](https://github.com/provables/sequencelib/blob/main/Sequencelib/Synthetic/A005/A005206.lean)), and atlas-lean's Conway-like predicate. **No proof assistant holds a totality proof for any relative where totality takes work.** That rules out V, Conolly, Tanny, Fox's B, Deane–Gentile's diluted recurrence, Golomb's 3, 2, 1 start and Q̃. Conway's slowness and asymptotics are unformalized too. These negatives rest on searches of GitHub, arXiv, OEIS, the Lean Zulip archive and lab sites. Private or unindexed work cannot be ruled out.

## Relatives fall only to slowness, including 2026's perturbed Q

Every proof that a relative survives exploits hidden regularity. Fox notes that the sequences he could analyze "have either an interleaved or slow structure". He adds that "it is theoretically possible that, after a very long initial condition, the Hofstadter Q-sequence eventually becomes interleaved or slow", which he calls "an unlikely, but remote, possibility" ([Fox thesis](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/Theses/NathanFoxThesis.pdf)). Proofs that a sequence dies, by contrast, are routine finite or symbolic computations. The table collects the relatives whose fate is settled.

The classical relatives show how small changes tame the recurrence. Conway's sequence has trivial totality: Mallows and Kubo–Vakil each note c(n) ≤ n "so that c(n) is well-defined by the recurrence" ([Pelesko 2004](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL7/Pelesko/pel11.pdf)). Its depth lies in the asymptotics behind Conway's famous prize, proved over 16 and 28 pages ([Mallows 1991](https://doi.org/10.2307/2324028); [Kubo–Vakil](https://zbmath.org/?q=an:0852.05010)). Shifting a lookback by one, as Conolly's and Tanny's sequences do, makes slowness provable at olympiad level. The 1990 Canadian Mathematical Olympiad asked for it, and Jackson and Ruskey give a one-page tree argument ([CanMO 1990](https://imomath.com/othercomp/Can/CanMO90.pdf); [Jackson–Ruskey](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0504400)).

The closest unshifted relatives took longer. V(n) = V(n − V(n−1)) + V(n − V(n−4)), started from four 1s, was observed to be slow by Hofstadter and Huber in 1999. Their unpublished proof was "a long, tedious, case by case tracking down of many branches of cases and sub-cases". Balamohan, Kuznetsov and Tanny gave a **3.5-page simultaneous induction** in 2007, and totality follows from it at once ([BKT 2007](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Tanny/tanny3.pdf)). Allouche and Shallit later proved that V's *frequency* sequence is 2-automatic (computable by a finite automaton reading n in base 2), but left open whether V's differences are ([arXiv 1103.1133](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.1133)).

Fox's three-term B, started from 1..5, is slow by an induction of about ten pages. Fox chose it because, apart from V and sequences built from V, every known slow sequence had a shift or an inner positive coefficient ([arXiv 1611.08244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08244)). Deane and Gentile's diluted recurrence keeps one of Q's two nested terms: q(n) = q(n − q(n−1)) + f(n). They prove the sequence exists for every slow forcing term f with f(1) = 0, a condition they stress is sufficient but not necessary ([arXiv 2311.13854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13854); [follow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17764)). None of this transfers to Q. Setting f(n) = Q(n − Q(n−2)) would require that term to be slow, and it is not.

### Two AI-assisted 2026 proofs settle the parity-perturbed Q, unverified

Marco Mantovanelli, an independent researcher, introduced Q̃(n) = Q̃(n − Q̃(n−1)) + Q̃(n − Q̃(n−2)) + (−1)ⁿ with Q̃(1) = Q̃(2) = 1 in March 2026. OEIS A394051 notes that **all terms are odd** and that the sequence survives to 3·10¹⁰ ([OEIS A394051](https://oeis.org/A394051)). His proof that it is well-defined went through three versions in four months.

Version 1 (31 March) claimed a finite-state proof built on a four-context "critical core". It called the argument "well-suited for formal verification in a proof assistant such as Lean or Coq" ([v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29622v1)). Benoît Cloitre then found a gap ([v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29622v2)). Version 2 (18 May) rebuilt the argument as "certified finite-state induction". Version 3 concedes that it "left a semantic gap: closure of the abstract overlap graph did not imply that every future recurrence position realized the required source context" ([v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29622v3)).

Version 3 (22 July) is an induction over "return words", the 13 block types into which an associated 7-bit code stream parses ([v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29622v3)). It begins with a reduction to a binary system and a reconstruction principle (Theorem 2.4). The core is a finite kernel of **13 return words, 92 synchronized states and 122 transition rules**, checked against 28,025 semantic windows. Ten parameterized loop schemas then extend the kernel to all levels, and a gluing argument fixes absolute positions in the sequence. Finally, a potential-function certificate shows that every recursive call looks back at least 38 blocks, so it only ever reads terms already computed. The trusted base explicitly includes "the short deterministic checkers" and the Python runtime. The Zenodo package is 223,189 bytes, needs only CPython ≥ 3.11, and "is not a Lean, Coq, Isabelle, or HOL formalization" ([Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21495335)). The archive's own status table lists proof-assistant formalization and independent external review as "not performed" ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/21495335)).

Cloitre's independent proof is now the standard reference. "The Mantovanelli-Hofstadter Sequence" (arXiv 2604.06237; v1 on 4 April, v3 on 22 July, 50 pages) proves three things: Q̃ is well-defined and unique, Q̃(n)/n → 1/2, and Q̃(n) = n/2 + O(n/√log n), an order that cannot be improved. It describes itself as "a direct self-contained proof without a finite-state certificate" ([Cloitre v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06237v3)). Totality needs only Sections 2–3, about ten pages ([arXiv 2604.06237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06237)). They split the sequence by parity into two coupled recurrences and build an explicit two-tape interleaving of binary "arch" words starting from 001011. Dyck-path invariants show that the interleavings fit exactly, and an arch-by-arch induction runs from a ten-row base table.

Mantovanelli's later work credits Cloitre: "Cloître proved that the recursion is globally well-defined" ([arXiv 2603.16111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16111)). Neither paper has a journal reference ([arXiv API](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=2603.29622,2604.06237,2603.16111,2609.02412)). Both authors have already corrected substantive errors. Mantovanelli revised the main proof twice and removed "two incorrect ancillary statements" from a companion paper. Cloitre's version 3 supersedes a conditional constant from his first. That record is the strongest argument for machine-checking either proof.

Neither proof is a step toward Q, and both authors say so. The (−1)ⁿ term keeps every value odd, so Q̃(n+1) − Q̃(n−1) ∈ {0, 2} and each parity class is a slow sequence. This is the binary sequence s_n in Mantovanelli's reduction and the pair of slow sequences a(m), b(m) in Cloitre's parity split ([Cloitre v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06237v3)). Q takes both parities and has no such structure. Mantovanelli wrote that the perturbation "enables a finite-state reduction that does not appear to be available in the unperturbed case" ([v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29622v1)), and that "the classical Hofstadter Q-sequence remains substantially harder" ([v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29622v2)). Cloitre adds that "the corresponding conclusions remain open for Q" ([Cloitre v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06237v3)). Q̃ is therefore the newest case of the slow-structure pattern, not a crack in Q. It is still the closest relative ever proven total: it differs from Q only by the perturbation, with identical initial values and lookbacks.

## Five targets where a Lean project would be first

No realistic project will prove Q total, and the plan below does not pretend to. Its aim is to be first at things that are tractable, meaningful and not yet machine-checked, while avoiding four kinds of duplication. It does not restate the Erdős #422 variants, which formal-conjectures already carries. It does not re-formalize G, H, F_k or A076502, which are already done in Rocq and Lean. It does not prove Conway's trivial totality. And it does not race the 3·10¹⁰ record, which Lean's kernel trails by six orders of magnitude and which cannot prove totality anyway. The size estimates below are rough guesses from the research notes, not measurements.

| Order | Target | What would be first | Primary source | Rough size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Canonical totality statement in Hofstadter's convention, its equivalent forms, upstream `well_defined` | Public proofs that the ℕ+ predicate, ℕ predicate, padded and `Option` forms agree; addresses #6544 | [PR #5791](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/5791); [issue #6544](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/issues/6544) | Days |
| 2 | Golomb's conditional limit, plus liminf ≤ 1/2 ≤ limsup | First verified theorem about Q's behavior | [Fox thesis](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/Theses/NathanFoxThesis.pdf) | Small |
| 3 | Fox's death theorem for starts 1..N; survival of Golomb's start 3, 2, 1 | First formal death and survival theorems for the Q-recurrence | [arXiv 1807.01365](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01365); [A244477](https://oeis.org/A244477) | 0.4–0.7k lines |
| 4 | V-sequence slowness and totality | First formal totality proof for a Hofstadter–Huber sequence | [BKT 2007](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Tanny/tanny3.pdf) | 0.3–1k lines |
| 5 | Q̃ totality via Cloitre §§2–3 and Mantovanelli Theorem 2.4 | First machine-checked totality proof for a Q-type recurrence whose totality was open | [arXiv 2604.06237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06237); [arXiv 2603.29622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29622) | 3–8k lines |

**The first deliverable is a canonical, non-vacuous statement in Hofstadter's own convention.** This project's padded definition already compiles and matches A005185. Its docstring asserts, but nothing yet proves, that totality is "Equivalent to `∀ n ≥ 1, q n ≤ n`" ([QDefinition.lean](../experiments/QDefinition.lean)). The library would prove that equivalence and uniqueness of solutions. It would prove equivalence with formal-conjectures' ℕ+ predicate, including the fact that ℕ+ subtraction's clamping at 1 never fires under the predicate's side conditions, which an early audit run flagged as questionable ([audit run 84063d6942](https://github.com/tadamcz/fc-review-results/blob/main/data/84063d6942/files/ErdosProblems/422.json)). It would prove equivalence with the `isSome` form of an `Option`-valued guarded definition, the never-eventually-constant lemma, and the soundness of the Boolean prefix checkers. It would add one small lemma showing that the essay's f(n) = 1 padding makes the recursion trivially total, which documents why that convention cannot serve as the definition.

Together these would give the first public Lean library in which Q's totality is a single faithful `Prop`, with every standard form proven equivalent. Epoch's audit reports some of these lemmas, but only the eventual-constancy result is published ([Epoch audit record](https://github.com/tadamcz/fc-review-results/blob/main/data/aea251bb26/files/ErdosProblems/422.json)). Upstreaming them with an `erdos_422.variants.well_defined` statement would close the gap that #6544 identifies. It would also give formal-conjectures the existence-and-uniqueness pattern that the #423 formalization used to make its statements non-vacuous ([#423 Lean](https://github.com/baobingzhang/jsp-000348-erdos423-lean)). Formalization also forces a precision the literature skips. Fox remarks that the start 1, 2 gives "Hofstadter's sequence without the initial 1" ([Fox 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01365)). That holds only while Q(n) < n, a hypothesis a formal statement has to carry.

**The second target is the only mathematics really about Q: Golomb's theorem that a convergent Q(n)/n must tend to 1/2.** The proof is elementary and fits Mathlib's `Filter.Tendsto`, `liminf` and `limsup` API. Suppose the limit is c. If c lies strictly between 0 and 1, the lookbacks sit near (1 − c)n, which forces c = 2c(1 − c) and hence c = 1/2. If c = 1, the lookbacks are o(n), so Q(n) is o(n), a contradiction. If c = 0, Q at least doubles from one geometric window of indices to the next until it outgrows n.

The research notes behind this report also sketch a stronger statement that appears to be unpublished: a total Q satisfies liminf Q(n)/n ≤ 1/2 ≤ limsup Q(n)/n. That implies both Golomb's theorem and unboundedness. We checked the sketch by hand but found it nowhere in print. Golomb's manuscript survives only as an image scan, which we did not read. So formalizing the sketch would certify a small new result rather than reproduce a known one.

Alongside it, Fox's Theorem 1 is the best real mathematics on the Q-recurrence itself. It shows that the start 1..N dies for N = 8, 11, 12 and every N ≥ 14, after exactly N + 28 terms when N ≥ 21. The proof combines 28 terms computed symbolically for N ≥ 13, which suits `omega`, with three concrete deaths, the longest-lived (N = 8) dying after exactly 420 terms ([Fox 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01365)). Kernel `decide` should check the concrete cases in a second or two, judging by this project's measurements of 0.9 s for 300 terms and 2.9 s for 1,000 ([QKernelDecide.lean](../experiments/QKernelDecide.lean)). Paired with the period-3 proof that Golomb's start survives, it gives the first formal death theorem and the first formal survival theorem for the Q-recurrence under Hofstadter's convention. Both rest on a reusable library for the Q-recurrence with arbitrary initial values.

**The V-sequence is the right warm-up for the headline.** BKT's slowness proof is a self-contained induction over integer inequalities, 3.5 pages long, with a base table up to n = 20 ([BKT 2007](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL10/Tanny/tanny3.pdf)). Formalizing it gives the first machine-checked totality proof for any Hofstadter–Huber sequence. It also forces the project to build the lemma "slow plus base case implies total", which Q̃'s parity classes need. Other targets fit the same infrastructure, either in parallel or afterwards: Deane–Gentile's diluted theorem, Fox's B, and Conolly's olympiad-level slowness, which is absent from Compfiles ([compfiles](https://github.com/dwrensha/compfiles)).

**The headline is Q̃'s totality, following Cloitre's route, with Mantovanelli's reconstruction principle as the first milestone.** That theorem says that any binary solution of the clock and local recurrences, with the right four initial values, rebuilds Q̃ with every argument in [1, n−1]. It runs about a page, as does Cloitre's parity split, and both can be done before any word combinatorics ([v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29622v3)). The core is Cloitre's Sections 2–3: lists of booleans with a total interleaving function, Dyck height invariants, the exact-fit lemma and the arch-by-arch induction. Mantovanelli's finite kernel, about 20 KB of data plus a 1,200-term prefix, is small enough to replay with `decide` as a cross-check. His all-level gluing, where version 2 failed, is the expensive and risky part, so it should not be the primary route.

A completed proof would be the first machine-checked totality proof for any recurrence with Q's exact lookbacks and initial values whose totality had been open. It would also be the first independent validation of two unrefereed, AI-assisted proofs, which is exactly the step Mantovanelli's archive lists as not performed. Kernel-only checking should be the default. Cloitre–Ma–Zhang's Lean development is the model: it relies only on Lean's three standard axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`), with "no admitted proofs, extra axioms, or native decision axioms" ([arXiv 2609.33421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33421)). Q's totality itself should stay a stated `Prop`, never a theorem closed with `sorry`.

Timing matters. Cloitre wrote the standard Q̃ proof, and on 27 September 2026 he co-authored an AI-assisted Lean 4 formalization of a nested recurrence, so his group has the most obvious head start on formalizing Q̃. That is our inference, not anything they have announced. It argues for announcing the project early, on formal-conjectures issue #6544, on Lean Zulip and to the two authors. It also argues for doing targets 1–3 first, since they take days to weeks and nobody has claimed them. Agentic research queues have just started listing #422 ([MATH-PROGRAMME #1692](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1692)), so the statement-level contributions will not stay unclaimed for long either.

## Conclusion

The usual picture of AI and open problems does not fit Q. Proof search never had a fair chance. For ten months the formal target was an opaque constant, and today it is a set of implications that hold vacuously if Q dies, the most interesting of which is the totality question in disguise. The decisive AI contributions were audits that found these defects, not attempts on the mathematics. What is missing is an idea, not compute or tooling. Every survival proof in this family, the 2026 ones included, rests on hidden slow or interleaved structure, and no one has a comparable invariant for Q. Wolfram's essay suggests a more sobering reading: that Q's behavior is computationally irreducible, so a short invariant need not exist at all. Either way, any AI claim to have "solved" a task that embeds the problem, such as FVAPPS task 2638 or the `eventually_const` variant, should first be treated as a probable bug in the specification, not a breakthrough.

What is scarce around Q in 2026 is trust. Independent researchers working with ChatGPT and Claude are proving results about relatives faster than referees can check them, with their gaps and repairs on public record. Benchmark and conjecture repositories carry statements that no one has shown to be non-vacuous. Formal statements of Q have to choose among three conventions for nonpositive indices, and only one of them poses the real question. A Lean project could make Q's totality a single `Prop` in Hofstadter's convention with all its equivalent forms proven, verify the only theorems about Q's behavior, and machine-check the closest relative ever proven total. That project would be the first to turn this year's AI-assisted mathematics about Q into verified knowledge. It would also leave behind the infrastructure for slowness arguments, reconstruction and certificates that any future invariant for Q would need.
